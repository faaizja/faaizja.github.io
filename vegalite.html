Data-Driven Insights

Exploring video game sales data through interactive Vega-Lite visualizations.

Visualization 1

Global Sales by Genre and Platform

Prompts:

Prompt 1: What single combination of game platform and genre (e.g., Wii Sports games vs. PS3 Action games) has generated the highest total global sales?

Prompt 2: Focusing on a specific console generation (Wii, PS3, and Xbox 360), how do the global sales of different genres compare to one another?

Reflection

I chose a grouped bar chart filtered to the console generation of the Wii, PS3, and X360, to clearly compare genre performance. This design choice avoids cluttering the screen with too many platforms or indications that one might get confused with. The visualization clearly reveals that action games dominate global PS3 sales, action games dominate XBOX 360 sales, and the Wii's massive success was driven by the sports genre.

Visualization 2

Sales Over Time by Platform and Genre

Prompts:

Prompt 1: How did the peak sales years of two rival consoles (PS3 and Xbox 360) compare over time?

Prompt 2: How has the total sales of the shooter genre evolved over time compared to the sports genre?

Reflection

I used a line chart to track sales over time, splitting PS3 and Xbox 360 into separate columns and coloring lines by genre. This allows for a clear comparison of peak years for each console and genre success. We can see that shooters steadily grew on both consoles, peaking around 2011-12, and nearly always being more popular than sports titles.

Visualization 3

Regional Sales vs. Platform

Prompts:

Prompt 1: How do total sales in Japan compare to North America and Europe across the generation of consoles: Wii, PS3, X360, DS, PSP?

Prompt 2: Which platform relies the most on sales outside of NA, EU, and JP for their total sales?

Reflection

To answer these prompts, I used a stacked bar chart comparing the top 7th-generation platforms. Because I used the wide CSV dataset file from the github, I implemented Vega-Lite's "fold" feature to collapse the four separate regional sales columns into a single region variable. The chart highlights regional differences with colors, such as the DS dominating the Japanese market while the Xbox 360 pretty much dominated North America and Europe.

Visualization 4

The East vs. West Divide

Prompts:

Prompt 1: Is there a distinct regional preference between North America and Japan when it comes to specific game genres?

Prompt 2: Do the highest-selling games globally tend to perform equally well in both Western and Eastern markets, or do they lean heavily to one side?

Reflection

For my visual story, I wanted to explore the cultural divide in gaming preferences using a scatter plot. By plotting North American sales against Japanese sales and coloring by genre, we can see a clear difference in popularity be region. We can see a distinct split: role-playing games pull heavily upward along the Y-axis (Japan), while shooters and action games stretch far along the X-axis (North America). A design choice I chose was to add interactive tooltips, which allows the user to see exactly which blockbuster titles are driving these regional trends.
